The Pathway

From preparation to demonstrated application.

ACDI progressively moves participants through preparation, simulation, and firsthand clinical immersion to demonstrated application and impact. Each stage serves a distinct function and cannot simply be replaced with a passive equivalent.

Tier 1

Preparation

Tier 1 does more than provide information. It prepares participants to know what to look for and understand what they are seeing.

  • Foundational knowledge informed by evidence.
  • Preparation materials that communicate the nature and intensity of participation, so participants understand expectations and limits.

Tier 2

Simulation

Simulation provides structured application. It introduces uncertainty, human response, time pressure, and situational complexity that drawings and classroom instruction abstract away.

  • Applied work in interdisciplinary cohorts, where different professions interpret the same situation differently.
  • Stress with a purpose: the value lies in what the conditions reveal, not in the stress itself.

Tier 3

Clinical Immersion

Immersion introduces authentic clinical complexity and unpredictable situational variability. The variables are no longer controlled.

  • Participants observe, document, interpret, and translate what they experience into professional decisions.
  • A consistent ACDI observation and documentation framework provides rigor even when individual clinical experiences differ.

Tier 4

Fellowship

Fellowship is the application and evidence stage of the pathway. It is not simply a more advanced course or another examination.

  • Intentional application of ACDI learning to professional work.
  • Disciplined documentation of the decisions and process.
  • Connection between observations, evidence, and professional interventions.
  • Evaluation of what occurred following implementation.
  • Capacity to contribute learning back to ACDI and the profession.

What to expect

“I didn’t understand this as well as I thought I did.”

For accomplished professionals, reaching this point is both an outcome and a prerequisite for transformational learning.

ACDI is demanding by design, and participants are likely to arrive with considerable experience. Part of the work is revealing the limits of what any one discipline can see on its own.

The program honors that commitment. ACDI creates psychological safety for professional vulnerability, so participants can take on high-intensity exposure and learn from it.

The credential

A credential that means demonstrated competence.

The ACDI credential signifies rigorous preparation, firsthand experiential and clinical learning, assessment, and demonstrated competence.

  • Tiers 1–3 form the integrated core leading to initial certification.
  • Attendance alone does not constitute completion, and incomplete participation does not receive certification.
  • Prior coursework, professional experience, or partial participation does not establish equivalency.

Fellowship

Process and judgment, not positive results alone.

Professional practice occurs within complex systems in which many variables are beyond an individual’s control. Fellowship therefore evaluates the rigor of the process, the quality of application, the use of evidence, professional judgment, and the evaluation of resulting outcomes.

Detailed Fellowship requirements will be developed as Tiers 1–3 mature.

Express interest

Common questions

What people ask first.

Who can participate?

Architects, designers, planners, engineers, equipment planners, facility professionals, healthcare administrators, clinicians, and other professionals who influence healthcare environments. ACDI deliberately cultivates multidisciplinary cohorts.

Do prior coursework or experience count toward the credential?

No. The credential requires completion and demonstrated competence across the required pathway. Prior coursework, professional experience, or partial participation does not establish equivalency.

Can simulation or clinical immersion be replaced with online learning?

No. Preparation, simulation, and firsthand clinical immersion each serve a distinct function. ACDI will not substitute passive or virtual education for required experiential and clinical components.

Is Fellowship required for initial certification?

No. Tiers 1–3 form the integrated core leading to initial certification. Tier 4 Fellowship is the later stage that demonstrates disciplined application of ACDI learning in professional practice.

Can a firm or employer sponsor participants?

Yes. Professional firms, healthcare organizations, and other employers may sponsor individual participants or multidisciplinary cohorts.

Can sponsors or product affiliates influence what ACDI teaches or finds?

No. No sponsor, affiliate, employer, or partner may purchase or improperly influence curriculum, assessment, credentialing, findings, endorsement, or Fellowship.

Is ACDI endorsed or accredited?

ACDI intends to pursue recognized healthcare-simulation standards, independent credential review, and validation from relevant professional societies. Those relationships will be announced as they are established.

What does it cost, and when does it begin?

Cost and dates have not been announced. ACDI is preparing its Alpha cohort, and you can express interest to hear more as details are confirmed.
